## Yes, it is possible in Canada

Surrogacy in Canada is legal, as long as it follows the Assisted Human Reproduction Act. There is no federal rule saying intended parents must be a couple. Single intended parents, including single men, work with clinics, surrogates and agencies across the country. Our FAQ [can single people have a baby through surrogacy in Canada?](/knowledge-centre/faqs/can-single-people-have-a-baby-through-surrogacy-in-canada) has the short answer.

A few honest caveats:

- **Some surrogates prefer to carry for a couple**, and some specifically love the idea of helping a single dad. Matching is about finding the right fit, not convincing someone.
- **Clinics have their own policies.** Most Canadian fertility clinics work with single intended fathers, but always ask.
- **Parentage law is provincial**, so the legal steps after birth depend on where the baby is born and where you live.

## The two journeys: egg donor and surrogate

As a single man, you will need two people to help you:

1. **An egg donor**, who provides the eggs
2. **A gestational surrogate**, who carries the pregnancy

In gestational surrogacy, the surrogate is not genetically related to the baby. The eggs come from a donor, are fertilized with your sperm through IVF, and the resulting embryo is transferred to your surrogate. Your baby will be genetically related to you and the egg donor.

Planning both at once can feel like a lot. The good news is they run on overlapping tracks, and the right team keeps them in sync. ## Step by step

Every journey is different, but a typical single father's path looks something like this:

1. **Free consult and planning.** Talk through your goals, budget and timeline.
2. **Choose a fertility clinic.** See [choosing a fertility clinic in Canada](/knowledge-centre/choosing-a-fertility-clinic-canada).
3. **Your own screening.** Semen analysis, infectious disease testing and usually genetic carrier screening. Because your sperm is going to a surrogate, it is generally treated under the same Health Canada safety rules as donor sperm, which often means freezing and a quarantine period before it can be used. Your clinic will explain the timing.
4. **Choose an egg donor.** This can be through an egg donation agency like Little Miracles, a frozen egg bank, or a known donor. See [how to choose an egg donor](/knowledge-centre/how-to-choose-an-egg-donor) and [fresh vs frozen donor eggs](/knowledge-centre/fresh-vs-frozen-donor-eggs).
5. **Create embryos.** The donor's eggs are retrieved (or frozen eggs are thawed) and fertilized with your sperm. Many dads choose to freeze all embryos and consider genetic testing. See [IVF step by step in Canada](/knowledge-centre/ivf-step-by-step-canada).
6. **Match with a surrogate.** This often happens alongside egg donation. See [how to find a surrogate in Canada](/knowledge-centre/how-to-find-a-surrogate-in-canada).
7. **Legal agreements.** A surrogacy agreement and an ovum donation agreement, each with independent legal advice for the surrogate and the donor.
8. **Embryo transfer and pregnancy.**
9. **Birth and parentage.** You complete your province's legal steps to be recognized as your baby's legal parent.

For a realistic sense of how long this takes, read [how long does surrogacy take in Canada?](/knowledge-centre/surrogacy-timeline-canada)

## What it costs

I believe in putting real numbers in front of you.

**Surrogacy:** a typical complete Canadian surrogacy journey runs about $80,000 to $120,000 CAD. The full breakdown is in [how much does surrogacy cost in Canada in 2026](/knowledge-centre/surrogacy-cost-canada-2026). That figure does not include egg donation.

**Egg donation:** through Little Miracles, the estimated total is $18,950 to $22,700 CAD, and higher for non-local donors. That includes:

| Item | Cost (CAD) |
|---|---|
| Agency fee | $2,750 to $3,500 + HST |
| Donor expenses (reimbursed, receipted) | $5,500 to $8,000 |
| Ovum agreement drafting | $2,000 |
| Donor independent legal advice | $900+ |
| OHSS coverage | $1,500 |
| Psychological evaluations | about $400 each |

Possible extras include travel and accommodation for a non-local donor, and satellite monitoring. See [egg donation costs in Canada](/knowledge-centre/egg-donation-costs-in-canada) for the details. Some clinic and IVF costs can overlap between the two journeys depending on how your clinic bills, so ask your clinic for one combined estimate.

**CSO agency programs** (CAD + HST, "from" prices): Independent Journey from $1,500, Foundation from $9,500, Guided Journey from $19,500, and Priority Match from $29,500. The agency fee is paid in three equal stages, never all upfront.

**Remember:** in Canada, surrogates and egg donors are never paid. Under the Assisted Human Reproduction Act, they can only be reimbursed for receipted expenses. See [how much do surrogates get paid in Canada?](/knowledge-centre/how-much-do-surrogates-get-paid-in-canada)

## Legal parentage as a single dad

Parentage is decided by provincial and territorial law. In general, after the birth, you take legal steps (which may be a declaration, a consent-based process or a court order, depending on the province) so you are recognized as the baby's only legal parent and your name goes on the birth registration.

Your lawyer will explain exactly what applies in your province and in the province where the baby is born. Read [parentage and birth registration after surrogacy](/knowledge-centre/parentage-and-birth-registration-after-surrogacy) and [who is on the birth certificate after surrogacy?](/knowledge-centre/faqs/who-is-on-the-birth-certificate-after-surrogacy) for the general picture.

## Planning like the only parent

Single dads are usually amazing planners, and here is where that pays off:

- **A will and guardianship.** If something happened to you, who would raise your child? This matters even before the baby is born. See [wills, guardianship and insurance for intended parents](/knowledge-centre/wills-guardianship-and-insurance-for-intended-parents).
- **Life and disability insurance.** You are the only income your child will have.
- **Parental leave.** Intended parents through surrogacy may be eligible for EI parental benefits. See [EI parental leave for intended parents](/knowledge-centre/ei-parental-leave-for-intended-parents).
- **Your village.** Who is your backup at 3 a.m.? Who can drive you to the hospital when your surrogate goes into labour? Line these people up now.
- **The newborn weeks.** Many single dads arrange for a parent, sibling or close friend to stay with them for the first few weeks.
