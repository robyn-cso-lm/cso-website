Maybe it came up in a quiet moment in your doctor's office. Maybe it came after cycle after cycle that did not work. However you got here, someone has said the words "donor eggs," and something in you went very still.

If that is where you are right now, I want to start by saying this: what you are feeling is real, it makes sense, and you do not have to rush past it.

I grew up in this field. I have been in rooms with families since I was 4 years old, I was an egg donor five times myself, and I founded Little Miracles in 2007. I have sat with so many intended parents in this exact moment. Here is what I have learned about getting through it.

## This is a real loss

For a lot of people, especially those who always pictured carrying and raising a child who has "their eyes" or "their grandmother's laugh," hearing that you may need a donor is a loss. Not of a child, but of a picture you held for a long time.

You may grieve:

- The genetic link you imagined
- The idea that your child would look like you or your family
- The version of pregnancy and parenthood you planned
- A sense of control over your own body

Sometimes people feel they are not "allowed" to grieve this, because nobody died and because there is still a path to a baby. Please hear me: you are allowed. To grieve is to know love. The grief you are feeling comes from how much you already love the child you have not met yet.

## Feelings you might not expect

Families have shared all of these with me. If you recognize yourself, you are not alone.

- **Guilt.** "My partner could have had a biological child with someone else."
- **Jealousy.** Of friends who got pregnant easily, or even of the donor.
- **Relief.** Finally, a plan that might actually work after so much trying.
- **Numbness.** Just being tired of feeling things.
- **Worry about bonding.** "Will I feel like the real mom?"

None of these make you a bad parent. They make you a human being going through something hard.

## Will I bond with my baby?

This is the question I hear most, so let me be honest with you.

Love does not live in DNA. It lives in the 3 a.m. feeds, the first giggle, the scraped knees and the bedtime stories. I have watched countless families through this, and the parents who worried most about bonding are often the ones who are the most fiercely, tenderly attached.

If you carry the pregnancy, you will also be the one growing this baby, feeling every kick and nourishing them for months. Your clinic can talk with you about the science of pregnancy and what it does and does not mean. What I can tell you from experience is that your child will know exactly who their parent is.

## Things that can help

Everyone's path through this is different, but here are a few things families have found helpful.

**Give yourself time.** You do not have to choose a donor next week. Many families take some time to feel ready, and that time is not wasted.

**Talk to a counsellor.** Fertility counselling is part of the donor egg process in Canada, and it is also just plain helpful. A counsellor who understands third party reproduction can help you sort through feelings you cannot quite name.

**Talk with your partner, honestly.** Partners often process this differently. One might be ready to move forward while the other needs to grieve. Both are okay. Try to make space for each other.

**Find people who have been there.** Hearing from other donor egg families can be a huge comfort. Ask your clinic or the Little Miracles team about connecting with others.

**Learn about the process.** For some people, knowledge brings calm. Reading about how donor matching works, what profiles look like, and what the steps are can turn a scary unknown into a plan. [Everything You Need to Know About Egg Donation in Canada](/knowledge-centre/egg-donation-in-canada) is a good place to start.

## Making peace with the donor

Some intended parents feel complicated about the donor at first. That usually softens with time and especially once you read a donor's personal statement.

The women who donate in Canada do it because they want to help. Egg donors in Canada cannot be paid for their eggs; they are only reimbursed for expenses. They go through screening, injections and a procedure for a family they may never meet. I have been that donor. I can tell you we think about "our" families with so much love and hope.

When you are ready, [How to Choose an Egg Donor](/knowledge-centre/how-to-choose-an-egg-donor) walks through what to look for in a profile.

## Thinking about your future child's story

You do not need to have every answer today, but it can help to know that families who are open with their children about donor conception often find it becomes a natural, warm part of the family story. Children tend to do best when they grow up knowing.

I wrote a whole piece about this: [Talking to Your Child About Egg Donation](/knowledge-centre/talking-to-your-child-about-egg-donation). You might want to save it for later.

## When you are ready, not before

There is no deadline on grief. Some families feel ready to move forward in a few weeks. Others take months. Some go back and forth. You will know when the hope starts to feel bigger than the sadness.

And when it does, we will be here. Not to rush you, but to walk with you.
