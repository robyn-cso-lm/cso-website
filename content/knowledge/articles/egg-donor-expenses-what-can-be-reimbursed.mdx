One of the first questions I get from both donors and intended parents is about money. Donors want to know if helping a family will cost them out of pocket. Intended parents want to know what they are expected to cover and how to do it the right way.

Here is the short version: in Canada, egg donors cannot be paid for their eggs, but they can be reimbursed for real expenses connected to the donation, with receipts.

Let me unpack what that means in everyday terms.

*This article is general information, not legal advice. The rules around reimbursement come from federal law and regulations, and both donors and intended parents should get independent legal advice before signing an agreement.*

## The law in plain language

Canada's *Assisted Human Reproduction Act* (AHRA) makes it illegal to buy eggs from a donor or to offer to. That means no "compensation," no "donor fee," no "thank you bonus" and no gifts meant as payment.

What the law does allow is reimbursement of expenditures the donor actually incurs because of the donation, as long as they are documented with receipts and handled according to the regulations.

So the question is never "how much does a donor get?" The question is "what did this donation actually cost the donor, and can she show it?"

You can read a quick overview in [Can egg donors be paid in Canada?](/knowledge-centre/faqs/can-egg-donors-be-paid-in-canada)

## Expenses that are commonly reimbursed

Every donation is different and every agreement is different, but these are the kinds of costs that often come up:

- **Travel.** Mileage, gas, transit, parking, and flights or trains if the clinic is far away
- **Accommodation and meals** when travel requires an overnight stay
- **Medications and supplies** that are not covered by another plan
- **Medical costs** related to the donation that are not otherwise covered
- **Childcare** while the donor attends appointments or recovers
- **Phone, courier or other small costs** directly tied to the donation
- **Legal advice** for the donor's own independent lawyer
- **Counselling** related to the donation

There is also a specific rule in the regulations about **lost work income**. In some situations a donor may be reimbursed for lost wages, but it requires the right medical documentation and must follow the rules closely. This is one area where you really want your lawyers involved before anything is promised.

Whether a specific expense qualifies, and how much documentation is needed, is something to confirm with your lawyers. I am not going to give you a list of dollar limits, because the right answer depends on the actual costs and the agreement.

## What is not allowed

To keep everyone safe and on the right side of the law:

- No lump sum payment that is not tied to actual receipts
- No "estimated" or "average" amounts in place of real expenses
- No paying for things that have nothing to do with the donation
- No gifts, gift cards or "extras" meant as payment for eggs
- No side arrangements outside the agreement

If anyone ever suggests a cash bonus, a "higher rate" for certain donors, or a deal that skips the paperwork, please walk away and talk to your lawyer. It puts the donor, the intended parents and the agency at risk.

## For donors: how to keep good records

I was an egg donor five times, so I know how easy it is to lose a parking receipt in the bottom of a bag. A few simple habits make reimbursement smooth:

- **Keep every receipt.** Snap a photo the moment you get it.
- **Write down mileage** with the date, where you went and why.
- **Use one folder or one envelope** for everything donation related.
- **Ask before you spend** if you are not sure something is covered.
- **Submit regularly**, not all at the end, so nothing gets lost.

Your Little Miracles coordinator can tell you how and when to submit.

## For intended parents: what to expect

As an intended parent, you will typically cover the donor's eligible expenses, along with clinic costs, legal fees and agency fees. In our programs, donor expenses are part of the overall budget we talk about up front, so you are not hit with surprises later.

For a full picture of what egg donation costs in Canada, including where donor expenses fit, see [Egg Donation Costs in Canada: What You Are Actually Paying For](/knowledge-centre/egg-donation-costs-in-canada).

A few tips for intended parents:

- **Expect receipts for everything.** That protects you too.
- **Do not offer extras.** I know you are grateful. A heartfelt card is lovely. A cash "thank you" is not allowed.
- **Let the process do the work.** When reimbursements run through a clear, documented system, it keeps the relationship warm and stress free.

## The ovum donation agreement

The ovum donation agreement is where the reimbursement rules for your donation get written down: what will be covered, how expenses are submitted and paid, and what happens if plans change. Both the donor and the intended parents should have their own independent lawyers review it.

You can learn more in [The Ovum Donation Agreement and Independent Legal Advice, Explained](/knowledge-centre/ovum-donation-agreement-canada).

## Why Canada does it this way

I grew up in this field, and I have seen how Canada's altruistic model shapes the whole experience. Donors who come forward here are doing it to help. That means the families they help can tell their children, with full honesty, that someone gave this gift because she wanted to.

It also means everybody has to be careful and organized about money. That is not a bad thing. It is part of doing this with integrity.
