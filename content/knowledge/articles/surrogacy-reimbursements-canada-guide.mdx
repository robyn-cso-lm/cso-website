# Surrogacy Reimbursements in Canada: Receipts, Expenses, and Escrow

The money side of a surrogacy journey can feel awkward to talk about. It should not be.

Clear, respectful reimbursement planning protects everyone. It helps a surrogate avoid being out of pocket for surrogacy-related costs, helps intended parents understand what they are funding, and creates a record that is organized rather than stressful.

This guide explains the practical framework. It is not a list of guaranteed eligible expenses or legal advice; the agreement, applicable law, and advice from qualified Canadian legal professionals govern each individual journey.

## Payment and reimbursement are not the same thing

Canada has an altruistic surrogacy framework. A surrogate cannot be paid a fee for carrying a pregnancy. At the same time, eligible surrogacy-related expenses may be reimbursed when the legal requirements are met.

That difference matters.

Reimbursement is intended to repay an actual, documented cost connected to the journey. It is not a salary, a flat monthly allowance, or a way to create a financial gain from the pregnancy.

## Why does everyone keep talking about receipts?

Because documentation is part of doing reimbursements properly.

In general, a reimbursement process needs a clear record of what the expense was, when it was incurred, what amount is being requested, and the supporting proof required for that expense. Health Canada’s framework also requires people making reimbursements to retain the required records.

The most useful habit is simple: keep records as you go. Trying to recreate months of appointments, parking, childcare, or pharmacy purchases at the end of a pregnancy is hard on everyone.

## What the paperwork often includes

The exact process varies by arrangement, but a well-run reimbursement file commonly includes:

- an itemized expense request or declaration;
- the date, amount, and reason for the expense;
- the receipt or other supporting record where required;
- any written recommendation or medical documentation where it applies;
- a record of the reimbursement being made; and
- a secure place to retain the documents.

For example, a routine expense request might include a dated receipt and a short note explaining that the cost was connected to an appointment or pregnancy-related need. The administrative system should make that clear, not make the surrogate feel as though she has to defend every reasonable request.

## Expense categories: ask before you assume

Canadian regulations set out categories of expenditures that may be reimbursed in relation to surrogacy. Depending on the circumstances, expenses can involve travel, parking, accommodation, childcare or dependant care, medication, records, legal services, counselling, and certain health-related products or services.

Whether a specific cost is appropriate depends on the facts, the agreement, and the required documentation. That is why the safest rule is not “someone online said it was allowed.” The safest rule is: **ask before incurring a cost you are unsure about, and keep the documentation.**

Some costs may need extra support, such as a written recommendation from an authorized healthcare professional. Reimbursement for certain work-related income loss has additional requirements. A lawyer or reimbursement professional familiar with the journey should guide those situations.

## What is escrow?

Escrow is a separate account or managed arrangement that holds money set aside for agreed surrogacy-related expenses.

It can make the process calmer because it separates reimbursement funds from day-to-day personal banking and creates a predictable workflow:

1. intended parent(s) fund the agreed account or arrangement;
2. the surrogate submits an itemized request and supporting records;
3. the request is reviewed under the agreed process; and
4. the reimbursement is paid and recorded.

Escrow is not a legal requirement in every journey, and different professionals use different systems. Its value is practical: clarity, timing, and a reliable paper trail.

## A simple reimbursement rhythm

There is no single right schedule, but many journeys work best with a consistent rhythm—for example, submitting requests weekly, biweekly, or monthly.

Consistency means fewer surprises. The surrogate knows when to submit records and when to expect reimbursement. Intended parents can see how the funds are being used. The coordinator, lawyer, or administrator is not trying to untangle a large backlog.

The goal is not to make a personal relationship feel transactional. It is to remove money stress so the relationship does not have to carry it.

## A practical “keep this” checklist

Keep copies of:

- receipts and invoices;
- appointment confirmations when useful to explain travel or childcare;
- mileage or travel records where applicable;
- pharmacy and medication records;
- childcare or dependant-care documentation;
- written medical recommendations when a product, service, or income-loss request requires one;
- submitted expense declarations; and
- confirmation that reimbursement was received.

If a receipt is unclear, flag it early rather than waiting. A quick question before reimbursement is much easier than trying to solve a missing-document issue months later.

## For intended parents: budget beyond one number

Reimbursements are not a fixed prize or a single line item that never changes. They reflect the real circumstances of a journey: travel distance, appointments, medications, family responsibilities, work situations, and pregnancy needs.

Build a thoughtful contingency fund and ask your professional team how reimbursements will be administered. Financial clarity is part of supporting a surrogate well.

## For surrogates: you should not have to carry the cost alone

Keeping records is important, but the process should still feel respectful and workable. You should know:

- who receives your expense requests;
- what format they prefer;
- when requests are processed;
- who can answer a question about a category or document; and
- what to do if an expense is urgent.

Ask those questions before the first expense occurs. A good process is one everyone understands.

## Start with the right questions

Before money changes hands, ask:

- How will reimbursements be submitted, reviewed, and paid?
- Will we use an escrow or managed account?
- What records should be kept for each type of expense?
- Who gives guidance when a cost is unusual or urgent?
- How are travel, childcare, and work absences handled?
- What does the agreement say, and when should we ask the lawyers for clarification?

## The bottom line

Reimbursements should be clear, documented, timely, and humane.

They are not a side issue. They are part of the practical care that allows a surrogate and intended parent(s) to focus on the relationship and the journey itself.
