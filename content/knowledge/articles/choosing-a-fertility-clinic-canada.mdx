Choosing a fertility clinic can feel like choosing a surgeon, a bank, and a second home all at once. You will spend a lot of time there. You will trust them with your embryos, your money, and some of the most hopeful and hardest moments of your life.

I grew up in this field, and I have worked alongside fertility clinics across Canada for my whole career. I am not going to tell you which clinic to choose, because the right one depends on you. What I can do is tell you what really matters, what to ask, and how to avoid being swayed by a shiny number.

*This article is general information, not medical advice. Your family doctor, and later your fertility doctor, can help you decide what is right for your situation.*

## Start with what you actually need

Before comparing clinics, get clear on your own situation. Different clinics are stronger in different areas. Ask yourself:

- Do I need straightforward IVF, or something more specialized, like genetic testing or recurrent loss care?
- Will we need an egg donor, sperm donor, or donated embryos?
- Will we need a gestational surrogate?
- Do I qualify for, and want to use, a provincially funded cycle?
- How far can I realistically travel for early morning appointments?

If you are not sure what you need yet, [Do I need an egg donor, a surrogate, or both?](/knowledge-centre/do-i-need-an-egg-donor-a-surrogate-or-both) is a good first read.

## How to read success data honestly

Most clinics share success numbers somewhere, and it is natural to want to pick the highest one. Please be careful.

**In Canada, there is a national data registry called CARTR Plus.** Canadian fertility clinics report their IVF cycles and outcomes to it, and national summaries are shared through the Canadian Fertility and Andrology Society. This is useful for understanding broad trends. Individual clinic results, however, are not always published in an easy side-by-side format, so you will often need to ask clinics directly.

When a clinic gives you a number, ask:

- **Success rate of what?** Pregnancy per transfer, live birth per transfer, or live birth per egg retrieval? These are very different numbers. Live birth is what you actually want to know about.
- **For which patients?** Results depend heavily on age and diagnosis. A clinic's overall rate may not reflect people like you. Ask for results in your age group, or for donor egg cycles, if that applies.
- **Over what time and how many cycles?** A high rate from a small number of cycles is less meaningful.
- **Who is included?** Some clinics may turn away more complex patients, which can make their numbers look better.
- **Single or multiple embryo transfers?** Transferring more embryos can raise pregnancy rates, but also raises the risk of twins and complications.

The most honest clinics will explain their data plainly and tell you what it means for you. That honesty is a good sign in itself.

## Location matters more than people think

IVF involves a lot of appointments, often early in the morning and sometimes with only a day's notice. When comparing clinics, think about:

- **Travel time** for monitoring visits.
- **Satellite monitoring.** Some clinics let you do bloodwork and ultrasounds closer to home and only travel for retrieval and transfer.
- **Weekend coverage.** Ovaries do not take weekends off.

In surrogacy, location involves two households. Your surrogate may live in a different city or province from you. Many surrogacy journeys work beautifully with travel for transfer and local monitoring for the surrogate. Our FAQ on [whether the surrogate needs to live near the intended parents](/knowledge-centre/faqs/does-a-surrogate-need-to-live-near-the-intended-parents) explains more.

## Wait times and funding

Wait times vary a lot across Canada. Some clinics have longer waits for a first consult. In provinces that fund IVF, there may be a separate waitlist for a funded cycle, and you may be able to start privately paid treatment sooner. Ask each clinic:

- How long until a first consult?
- Do you offer funded cycles, and what is the current wait?
- Can I start testing while I wait?

Read [which provinces help pay for IVF](/knowledge-centre/faqs/which-provinces-fund-ivf-in-canada) for the bigger picture, and remember that funding rules can change.

## If you need a surrogate or donor

This is where clinic choice really matters for our families. Not every clinic works with third-party reproduction, and those that do may have different policies. Ask:

- **Do you work with gestational surrogates?** How many surrogacy cycles do you do a year?
- **What screening do you require for surrogates and partners?** See [what happens at a surrogacy screening appointment](/knowledge-centre/surrogacy-screening-appointment-canada).
- **How do you handle Health Canada's safety rules** for donor eggs, donor sperm, and embryos created for a surrogate? How does that affect my timeline?
- **Do you work with egg donors from outside agencies,** or only your own program? Can you work with frozen donor eggs?
- **Do you accept embryos shipped from another clinic?** See [shipping embryos and gametes in Canada](/knowledge-centre/shipping-embryos-and-gametes-canada).
- **Do you work with international intended parents?**
- **What are your policies on embryo transfer numbers, PGT-A, and mosaic embryos?** Our article on [PGT-A embryo testing](/knowledge-centre/pgt-a-embryo-testing-explained) explains why this matters.

A clinic with strong surrogacy experience will have clear answers to these questions and a team that knows how to coordinate between intended parents, surrogates, lawyers, and agencies. Our [who does what in a Canadian surrogacy journey](/knowledge-centre/who-does-what-canadian-surrogacy-journey) article shows how all the pieces fit.

## Costs and transparency

Ask for a written fee schedule that covers:

- Consult and testing
- The IVF cycle itself
- Medications (often billed separately, through a pharmacy)
- ICSI, embryo freezing, PGT-A, and storage
- Frozen embryo transfer cycles
- Third-party extras, such as surrogate screening or donor coordination

Prices vary between clinics, so compare the full picture, not just the headline cycle fee. If you are doing surrogacy, see [how much surrogacy costs in Canada](/knowledge-centre/surrogacy-cost-canada-2026) to understand how clinic costs fit into the overall budget of roughly $80K to $120K CAD.

## How it feels matters too

After the numbers, trust your gut:

- Did they listen?
- Did they answer your questions in plain words?
- Do you know who to call when something goes wrong?
- Did the nurses treat you like a person?

You will be spending a lot of emotional time with this team. Feeling respected is not a luxury.
