You are planning for a baby, and part of that planning is the very practical question: how do we take time off work to be with them? If you are an intended parent, you may have heard that "maternity leave" does not apply to you. If you are a surrogate, you may be wondering whether you get any leave at all, since you will not be taking a baby home.

Both questions have real answers. Here is how Employment Insurance (EI) works in surrogacy, in plain language.

*This article is general information, not legal, tax or employment advice. EI rules change, and Service Canada decides every claim. Check the current rules on canada.ca or call Service Canada, and talk to your employer about job-protected leave.*

## Two different things: benefits and leave

People often mix these up:

- **EI benefits** are the money. They are a federal program run through Service Canada (except in Quebec, which has its own plan, more on that below).
- **Job-protected leave** is your right to take time off and come back to your job. That comes from employment standards law, which is provincial for most workers and federal for federally regulated workplaces like banks and airlines.

You usually need both: the leave so your job is safe, and the benefits so you have income while you are off.

## EI maternity benefits: for the surrogate

EI maternity benefits are for the person who is pregnant or has recently given birth. In surrogacy, that is the surrogate. Service Canada's own guidance says surrogates can receive maternity benefits.

In general terms:

- Maternity benefits can provide up to 15 weeks of income support.
- They can start up to 12 weeks before the due date, or on the date of birth if later.
- To qualify, you generally need enough insurable hours of work in the qualifying period (usually 600 hours), along with the other EI rules.

This gives a surrogate time to recover physically from pregnancy and birth, which matters. Pregnancy is pregnancy, and recovery is real. Read more in [postpartum recovery for surrogates](/knowledge-centre/postpartum-recovery-for-surrogates).

## EI parental benefits: for the intended parents

EI parental benefits are for parents caring for their newborn or newly adopted child. Surrogates are **not** eligible for parental benefits, because they are not the ones caring for the baby.

Intended parents who qualify for EI can generally claim parental benefits once the baby is born and in their care. You choose one of two options, and the choice applies to both parents:

| Option | Total weeks if shared | Max for one parent | Benefit rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Standard | up to 40 weeks | 35 weeks | 55% of average weekly insurable earnings, up to a yearly maximum |
| Extended | up to 69 weeks | 61 weeks | 33% of average weekly insurable earnings, up to a yearly maximum |

There is a one-week waiting period. The extra weeks only come when both parents share, which encourages both of you to take time at home.

Things to know for surrogacy specifically:

- **Paperwork.** Service Canada may ask for documents showing the child's birth and that you are the parents caring for the baby. If parentage is still being finalized in your province, keep your lawyer's letters and the hospital paperwork handy. See [parentage and birth registration after surrogacy](/knowledge-centre/parentage-and-birth-registration-after-surrogacy).
- **Timing.** Parental benefits must be used within a set window after the birth (longer for the extended option). Plan with your partner so neither of you runs out of time.
- **Self-employed parents** are not covered automatically. You can opt into EI special benefits, but you have to register in advance and wait a set period before you can claim, so look into this early.

## The new benefit for adoptive and surrogacy parents

For years, adoptive parents and parents through surrogacy received fewer total weeks than birth families, because they had no access to the 15 weeks of maternity benefits. The federal government recognized this gap.

In June 2024, Parliament passed legislation creating a new 15-week shareable EI benefit for parents through adoption or surrogacy, along with matching job-protected leave for federally regulated workers. Once it takes effect, it is meant to bring these parents up to the same total number of weeks as birth families when combined with parental benefits.

As of early 2026, the federal government said the start date had not yet been set and that work was underway to put it in place. **Before you plan your leave, check canada.ca or call Service Canada to confirm whether the new benefit is in effect** and how to apply. Provincial employment standards may also need to change before the extra leave is protected for workers outside federally regulated industries, so ask your employer too.

## Quebec: QPIP instead of EI

If you live and work in Quebec, maternity and parental benefits come from the Quebec Parental Insurance Plan (QPIP), not federal EI. QPIP has its own rules, benefit rates and number of weeks.

Quebec also reformed its surrogacy law in 2023, and QPIP has been updated over time to reflect families formed through surrogacy. Because the rules differ from the rest of Canada, check directly with QPIP (through the Quebec government) about what you qualify for. Our article on [surrogacy for Quebec families](/knowledge-centre/surrogacy-for-quebec-families) explains the legal side.

## Employer top-ups

Many employers offer "top-up" payments on top of EI for maternity or parental leave. Some policies were written before anyone thought about surrogacy, and may only mention birth or adoption. If yours is unclear:

- Ask HR in writing how the policy applies to parents through surrogacy.
- If you are a surrogate, ask whether the maternity top-up applies to you.
- Get the answer in writing well before the due date.

You may be the first person at your workplace to ask. That is okay. You are helping the next family too.

## For surrogates: lost wages are separate

EI maternity benefits are not the same as reimbursement of lost wages under your surrogacy agreement. Under Canada's reimbursement rules, a surrogate can be reimbursed for lost work income during pregnancy only with a written medical certification. Your agreement and your lawyer will explain how EI and reimbursements fit together so nothing is double counted. See [life and health insurance for surrogates](/knowledge-centre/life-and-health-insurance-for-surrogates) and [will surrogacy affect my job or maternity leave?](/knowledge-centre/faqs/will-surrogacy-affect-my-job-or-maternity-leave)

## A planning checklist

**Intended parents**
- Confirm you each have enough insurable hours.
- Decide standard or extended together.
- Check whether the new surrogacy benefit is in effect.
- Talk to HR about leave and top-ups.
- Know where your parentage paperwork will come from.

**Surrogates**
- Confirm your insurable hours.
- Talk to HR about maternity leave and any top-up.
- Ask your lawyer how lost wages and EI interact in your agreement.
