One of the most common worries I hear from intended parents goes something like this: "It is our baby, but it is not our body. How do we feel like part of this pregnancy without crossing a line?"

That worry tells me you are going to be good at this. Wanting to be involved and wanting to respect your surrogate are not opposites. With a little planning, you can have both. I grew up in this field, and the happiest journeys I have seen are the ones where everyone talked openly, early, about what involvement would look like.

## Start with a simple truth: it is her body, and your baby

In Canada, the surrogate keeps full control over her own body and her own medical care during the pregnancy. Her health information is hers, and her doctors and midwives need her consent to share it or to let you in the room. Your surrogacy agreement will usually describe how information and decisions are shared, and both of your lawyers will explain it before you sign. You can read the basics in [what is a surrogacy agreement](/knowledge-centre/faqs/what-is-a-surrogacy-agreement).

At the same time, this is your baby, and most surrogates genuinely want you to feel connected. In my experience, surrogates who were matched well love sharing this with the parents. The goal is to find the rhythm that works for all of you.

## Talk about it before the transfer

The best time to plan involvement is before anyone is pregnant. During matching, and again before the transfer, sit down together (in person or on video) and talk through:

- Which appointments the surrogate is comfortable having you attend
- How often you will check in, and how (text, calls, video, a shared photo album)
- Who shares news with whom, and when (for example, the beta result and the first heartbeat)
- When it is okay to tell friends, family or social media
- What the surrogate needs from you, and what you need from her
- How everyone wants to handle hard news, if it comes

Some families write this down as a simple "communication plan." It does not need to be formal. It just needs to be clear, and it should be revisited as the pregnancy goes on. Feelings change, and that is normal.

## Appointments and ultrasounds

There are a few moments most intended parents dream about:

- **The first ultrasound** at the fertility clinic, when you may see a heartbeat for the first time
- **The anatomy scan** in the middle of the pregnancy
- **Prenatal visits** where you can hear the heartbeat on a Doppler

Many surrogates are happy to have intended parents at some or all of these, as long as everyone respects the clinic or hospital's rules about visitors. A few practical things to know:

- Policies about how many people can come into an ultrasound room, and whether recording is allowed, vary by clinic and hospital. Ask ahead.
- Some internal exams are private. It is very normal for a surrogate to want her parents in the room for the ultrasound but not for the pelvic exam. Step out with a smile.
- If you cannot attend, ask whether a video call or a photo of the scan is possible.
- Her care provider (an OB, family doctor or midwife) works for her. They will often be welcoming to you, but she is the patient.

For what the early months look like medically, read [from positive test to OB care](/knowledge-centre/positive-pregnancy-test-to-ob-care-surrogacy).

## Everyday updates

Between appointments, small updates keep you feeling connected without wearing anyone out. Ideas that families love:

- A weekly bump photo
- A quick text after each appointment
- Voice notes or videos of the baby kicking later in the pregnancy
- A shared online album or private group chat
- Recorded messages from you that she plays near her belly, so baby hears your voices

Agree on how fast replies are expected. A surrogate who works full time and has kids may not answer within the hour, and that is not a sign anything is wrong.

## Boundaries that protect the relationship

Healthy boundaries are not cold. They are what keep the relationship warm for nine months. A few that come up often:

- **Food, exercise and lifestyle.** Your surrogate agreed to follow medical advice and the terms of your agreement. Beyond that, constant questions about what she ate today can feel controlling. If you have a real concern, raise it kindly, or talk to your agency.
- **Her family time.** She has a partner, kids and a life. Dropping by uninvited is not okay, even if you are excited.
- **Her body and feelings.** Ask how she is doing, not only how the baby is doing. Surrogates notice.
- **Disagreements.** These happen in even the best matches. Your agency or a counsellor can help you talk through them before they grow.

## Staying involved from far away

Many intended parents live in another city, province or country. Distance does not have to mean disconnection. Our FAQ on [whether a surrogate needs to live near the intended parents](/knowledge-centre/faqs/does-a-surrogate-need-to-live-near-the-intended-parents) covers the basics. For long-distance families:

- Schedule regular video calls, maybe every week or two
- Join appointments by video when the clinic and surrogate allow it
- Send care packages, meals or small surprises to her home
- Plan one or two in-person visits, such as for the anatomy scan or a baby shower
- Talk early about travel plans for the birth, including how soon you can get there if baby comes early

International families have extra planning to do around travel and paperwork. Read [surrogacy in Canada for international intended parents](/knowledge-centre/surrogacy-for-international-intended-parents-canada).

## Toward the birth

As the due date gets closer, involvement shifts to planning for delivery day: who is in the room, who holds the baby first, skin-to-skin, feeding and the hospital stay. This deserves its own conversation, and we cover it in [surrogacy birth plans and delivery day](/knowledge-centre/surrogacy-birth-plan-and-delivery-day). If feeding with breast milk matters to you, read [breast milk and surrogacy](/knowledge-centre/breast-milk-and-surrogacy) early so you can raise it with your surrogate well ahead of time.
