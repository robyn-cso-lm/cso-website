If you are two dads dreaming of a baby, or a single parent ready to do this on your own, you have probably already figured out the big picture: you will need an egg donor, and you will need a surrogate. That can feel like twice the planning, twice the paperwork and twice the cost.

It is a lot. It is also very doable, and families like yours do it in Canada all the time. Let me show you how the egg donation piece fits in.

I grew up in this field, I was an egg donor five times myself, and I founded Little Miracles in 2007. Some of the families I treasure most are dads and solo parents who came to us with a dream and a very long list of questions. Bring yours.

*This article is general information, not legal or medical advice. Please speak with your fertility clinic and a fertility lawyer about your own situation.*

## Two journeys, one family

For gay male couples and many single parents, building a family usually means:

1. **An egg donor**, who provides the eggs
2. **Sperm** from one or both intended dads, or a sperm donor
3. **A gestational surrogate**, who carries the pregnancy but is not genetically related to the baby

The donor's eggs are fertilized in an IVF lab, the embryos are created, and an embryo is transferred to your surrogate. In Canada, the surrogate and the egg donor are almost always two different people. Our FAQ [Does the surrogate use her own egg?](/knowledge-centre/faqs/does-the-surrogate-use-her-own-egg) explains why.

If you are not sure what you need yet, read [Do I need an egg donor, a surrogate, or both?](/knowledge-centre/do-i-need-an-egg-donor-a-surrogate-or-both)

## Canada is a welcoming place to do this

Canadian surrogacy and egg donation are open to same-sex couples and single parents. You do not need to be married, and you do not need a partner. My article on [surrogacy for LGBTQ+ families in Canada](/knowledge-centre/lgbtq-surrogacy-canada) goes into more detail.

The same laws apply to every family. Under the *Assisted Human Reproduction Act*, it is illegal to pay an egg donor (or a surrogate). Donors are reimbursed only for receipted, donation-related expenses. That is true no matter who the intended parents are.

## Choosing a donor as two dads

This part is often more fun than people expect, and sometimes more emotional, too.

Some couples look for a donor who shares traits with the partner who is *not* providing sperm, so the child may reflect both of you. Others focus on shared culture, values or the donor's personal story. Some couples decide one partner will provide sperm for the first child, and the other for a sibling later.

There is no right approach, only what feels right to your family. Our guide on [how to choose an egg donor](/knowledge-centre/how-to-choose-an-egg-donor) walks through reading profiles in detail.

**A practical tip:** if you hope each dad might be a genetic father one day, talk with your clinic early about how embryos are created and stored. It can affect how you plan your donor cycle.

## Planning as a single parent

If you are doing this solo, first, I am cheering for you.

Single parents use the same steps: donor, sperm (your own or a donor's), IVF and a surrogate. What I see make the biggest difference for solo parents is support. Think ahead about who will be with you at appointments, at the birth and in those first sleepless weeks. A strong village makes the whole journey feel lighter.

## Fresh or frozen?

Many families combining egg donation with surrogacy create embryos first and freeze them, then transfer to the surrogate when she is ready. Whether you use a fresh donor cycle or frozen donor eggs is a decision to make with your clinic. I wrote about the general trade-offs in [Fresh vs frozen donor eggs](/knowledge-centre/fresh-vs-frozen-donor-eggs).

## What egg donation costs

Here is the honest number for the egg donation side through Little Miracles. Your clinic's IVF fees and your surrogacy costs are separate.

| Item | Estimated cost (CAD) |
|---|---|
| Agency fee (non-refundable, + HST) | $2,750 to $3,500 |
| Donor expenses (reimbursements) | $5,500 to $8,000 |
| Ovum donation agreement | $1,350 to $1,550 |
| Donor independent legal advice | $550 |
| Psychological evaluations | About $400 each |
| **Estimated total for this part** | **$16,450 to $20,400** |

Costs are usually higher for a non-local donor. Every situation is different, so we will give you a number for your own plan. A [shared-cost cycle](/knowledge-centre/shared-cost-egg-donation-canada) is also available at Little Miracles, and may help lower costs for some families.

For the full breakdown, see our [egg donation costs guide](/knowledge-centre/egg-donation-costs-in-canada).

## The surrogacy side

Your surrogacy journey has its own costs, timeline and legal agreement. At Canadian Surrogacy Options, programs start from $1,500 (Independent Journey), $9,500 (Foundation), $19,500 (Guided Journey) and $29,500 (Priority Match) CAD plus HST, with the agency fee paid in three equal stages. You can estimate your total with our [surrogacy cost calculator](/surrogacy-costs).

When the two journeys are planned together, timelines can line up better. For example, you can be working on creating embryos while you are being matched with a surrogate.

## The legal side

You will have two separate agreements: an [ovum donation agreement](/knowledge-centre/ovum-donation-agreement-canada) with your donor, and a surrogacy agreement with your surrogate. Each person has their own lawyer.

How parentage is recognized, and how both dads or a single parent end up on the birth certificate, depends on the province. A fertility lawyer in your province will guide you through it. Our FAQ [Who is on the birth certificate after surrogacy?](/knowledge-centre/faqs/who-is-on-the-birth-certificate-after-surrogacy) is a good starting point.
