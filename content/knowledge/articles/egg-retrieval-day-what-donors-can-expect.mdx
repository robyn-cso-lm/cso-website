If you are reading this, you have probably already done the hard part. You applied, you got through screening, you did the injections and the early morning monitoring appointments. Now retrieval day is on the calendar and your brain has decided to imagine every possible scenario at 2 a.m.

I get it. I was an egg donor five times, and I still remember the butterflies before my first retrieval. So let me walk you through what the day usually looks like, so the only surprises are good ones.

*This article is general information, not medical advice. Your fertility clinic will give you instructions for your own cycle, and their instructions always come first.*

## The trigger shot: your countdown starts

A day or two before retrieval, your clinic will tell you it is time for your trigger shot. This injection helps your eggs finish maturing so they are ready to be collected.

The timing of this shot really matters. Your clinic will give you an exact time to take it, and your retrieval is scheduled around that time. Set two alarms. Ask your partner, roommate or best friend to set one too. If anything goes wrong with the trigger (you miss the time, you are not sure the full dose went in, the pen jams), call your clinic right away. They have heard it all before and they would much rather get a call than find out later.

## The night before

Your clinic will send you instructions, and they usually cover things like:

- **When to stop eating and drinking.** Most clinics ask you to fast before the procedure because you will be sedated. Follow their exact timing.
- **What to wear.** Comfy, loose clothes. Think sweatpants and a cozy hoodie, not jeans.
- **What to leave at home.** Jewellery, nail polish, perfume and contact lenses are often on the "please skip" list. Check yours.
- **Who is driving you home.** You cannot drive yourself after sedation, and most clinics will not let you leave alone. Line up your ride early.

Try to get some sleep. If you cannot, that is okay too. Nerves are normal, and the team you are about to meet does this every single day.

## When you arrive at the clinic

You will check in, change into a gown and meet the nurses who will take care of you. Someone will usually go over your health, confirm when you last ate or drank, and ask about allergies. You may get an IV for the sedation.

This is a great moment to ask any last questions. Nothing is too small. "Will I feel anything?" "How long will I be in recovery?" "When will I know how many eggs were retrieved?" Ask away.

## The retrieval itself

Here is the part most donors worry about, so I will be straightforward.

Egg retrieval is a short procedure. You are sedated, so most donors do not remember much of it. The doctor uses an ultrasound to guide a thin needle through the vaginal wall to the ovaries and gently collects the fluid from the follicles, where the eggs are. There are no stitches and no cuts on your belly.

Your clinic can tell you how long it usually takes there and what kind of sedation they use. Every clinic is a little different.

## Waking up and recovery

You will wake up in a recovery area with a nurse keeping an eye on you. Common things donors notice afterward include:

- **Cramping**, a bit like period cramps
- **Bloating** or a full, heavy feeling in the belly
- **Light spotting**
- **Grogginess** from the sedation

The nurses will tell you when you are ready to go home. Before you leave, you should get written aftercare instructions. Read them when you are home and less groggy, and keep them somewhere you can find them.

## The rest of retrieval day

Plan to do nothing. I mean it. This is a couch day.

- Rest, nap and let someone else handle dinner
- Follow your clinic's advice on pain relief (do not guess which medication is okay, ask)
- Keep a heating pad close if your clinic says that is fine for you
- Sip fluids, and eat something light when you feel ready

Most donors feel much better within a day or two, but everybody recovers at their own pace. Be kind to yourself.

## Signs you should call your clinic

This part is important, so please do not skip it.

Call your clinic right away, or go to emergency if you cannot reach them, if you have:

- Severe or worsening belly pain
- A lot of bloating that keeps getting worse
- Trouble breathing
- Nausea or vomiting that will not stop
- Very little urine, or very dark urine
- Fever, or heavy bleeding

These can be signs of a complication called ovarian hyperstimulation syndrome (OHSS) or something else that needs care. OHSS is uncommon, and your clinic watches for it closely throughout your cycle, but you should know the signs. You can read more in [What is OHSS?](/knowledge-centre/faqs/what-is-ohss)

## The days after retrieval

Your ovaries are still a bit enlarged for a little while after retrieval, which is why many clinics ask you to take it easy with exercise and to follow their guidance on sex and contraception until your next period. Your next period may come a little earlier or later than usual.

If you have expenses tied to your retrieval, like travel, parking or medications that were not covered, keep every receipt. In Canada, donors are reimbursed for documented expenses, not paid for their eggs. I wrote more about that in [What expenses can an egg donor be reimbursed for?](/knowledge-centre/egg-donor-expenses-what-can-be-reimbursed)

## The feelings nobody warns you about

Some donors feel proud and light right away. Some feel a little teary and are not sure why. Some feel nothing big at all and just want a nap and a snack. All of those are normal.

You just did something extraordinary for a family you may never meet. Let yourself feel whatever comes up. If you want to talk it through, the Little Miracles team is here, and so am I. I grew up in this field and I have been on your side of the table five times. You are never bothering us.

## Common questions

**Will I know how many eggs were retrieved?** Many clinics share that with donors, but policies differ. Ask your clinic or your Little Miracles coordinator.

**Does retrieval affect my future fertility?** This is a really common worry. Start with [Does egg donation affect future fertility?](/knowledge-centre/faqs/does-egg-donation-affect-future-fertility), and bring your own questions to your clinic.

**Can I donate again?** Some donors do. Your clinic will look at how your cycle went and talk with you about whether another cycle is right for you.
