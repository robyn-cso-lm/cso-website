Opening your first batch of egg donor profiles can feel like a lot. You are looking at a stranger's photos, her family history, her school marks and her hopes, and somewhere in there you are trying to picture your future child. Some people feel an instant spark. Others feel overwhelmed and a little guilty that they are "choosing" at all.

Both reactions are completely normal. Let me help you make sense of what you are reading.

I grew up in this field, I was an egg donor five times myself, and I founded Little Miracles in 2007. I have sat on both sides of the profile. Here is what I want every intended parent to know.

*This article is general information, not medical advice. Your fertility clinic is the right place for questions about your own medical situation.*

## First, what a donor profile is (and is not)

A donor profile is a snapshot. It usually includes:

- **Physical traits:** height, build, hair and eye colour, ethnicity and sometimes childhood and adult photos
- **Education and interests:** schooling, work, hobbies, talents
- **Personal statement:** in her own words, why she wants to donate and what she would want a future child to know
- **Medical and family history:** her own health, plus what she knows about her parents, siblings and grandparents
- **Reproductive history:** whether she has donated before or has children of her own
- **Screening status:** where she is in medical, genetic and psychological screening

What a profile is not: a guarantee. No profile can promise a pregnancy, a certain personality or a child who looks a certain way. Genetics is a shuffle, and your child will be their own person. I say that with love, because I have watched families get stuck chasing a "perfect" profile that does not exist.

## Start with what matters most to you

Before you scroll, sit down with your partner (or yourself, with a cup of tea) and write down your top three priorities. Not ten. Three.

For some families it is a shared ethnic or cultural background. For others it is height, or education, or a donor who is open to future contact. Some families care most about the personal statement and simply want to feel a connection to the person who is helping them.

There are no wrong answers here. But if you go in without priorities, every profile will feel either "almost right" or "not quite," and that is exhausting.

## Reading the medical and family history

This is the part I want you to slow down on.

Look at the donor's own health and her family history together. A single condition in an extended relative is not automatically a red flag, and a "clean" history is not automatically a guarantee. Family histories are only as complete as what the donor knows.

Here is what I suggest:

- **Make a list of anything you have questions about** and bring it to your fertility clinic or a genetic counsellor. They can tell you what is relevant to your own situation.
- **Ask about genetic carrier screening.** If you are using sperm from a partner or a donor, your clinic may want to compare carrier results so you understand any shared risks.
- **Do not self-diagnose from a profile.** I have seen intended parents panic over something that turned out to be minor, and miss asking about something that mattered. Let the professionals help you read it.

## Proven donors and first-time donors

Some profiles will say a donor has donated before. Some intended parents feel more comfortable with a donor who has completed a previous cycle, because there is some history to look at.

A first-time donor is not a "lesser" choice. Every donor was a first-time donor once (including me). Your clinic can talk with you about what a donor's history does and does not tell you, and how her screening results fit your plan. I will not quote numbers or outcomes here, because every cycle is different and your clinic is the right source for that conversation.

## Known donor or agency-matched donor?

You might already have a sister, cousin or friend who has offered. That is a beautiful thing, and it also comes with extra counselling and legal steps. If you are weighing that, read [Should we use a known or anonymous egg donor?](/knowledge-centre/faqs/known-donor-or-anonymous-donor) first.

If you are matching through an agency like Little Miracles, the profiles you see are from donors who have applied, been screened and been prepared for what donation involves.

## The personal statement is not fluff

I always tell families: read the personal statement twice.

This is the closest you will get to hearing her voice. Why does she want to help? What does she hope for the family? What would she say to a child who asks about her one day?

Many of the families I have worked with tell me that, years later, the personal statement is what they kept. It is what they read to their child. Physical traits matter, but the "why" often matters more than people expect.

## Openness and future contact

Ask early about what kind of contact the donor is open to. Some donors are open to future contact if the child wants it. Others prefer to remain anonymous. Your [ovum donation agreement](/knowledge-centre/ovum-donation-agreement-canada) is where these expectations get written down, so it helps to know what you want before you get there.

If you are thinking about how you will one day share this story with your child, I wrote a whole piece on that: [Talking to your child about egg donation](/knowledge-centre/talking-to-your-child-about-egg-donation).

## A few honest cautions

- **Do not let a photo do all the deciding.** Photos are lovely. They are also one moment in time.
- **Do not wait forever for "the one."** Donors are not on hold indefinitely, and the right donor may be the one you keep coming back to, not the one who ticks every box.
- **Remember the law.** In Canada, donors cannot be paid for their eggs under the *Assisted Human Reproduction Act*. They are reimbursed only for receipted expenses. If anyone ever suggests a "bonus" or an under-the-table payment to secure a donor, walk away. You can read more in [Can egg donors be paid in Canada?](/knowledge-centre/faqs/can-egg-donors-be-paid-in-canada)
- **Budget for the whole picture.** Choosing a donor is one step. Our [egg donation costs guide](/knowledge-centre/egg-donation-costs-in-canada) lays out what the rest typically involves.

## Trust your gut, then check it

My honest advice? Use your priorities to narrow the list, use your clinic to check the medical side, and then trust your heart for the final choice. When families pick a donor they feel genuinely grateful for, that gratitude carries through the whole journey and into how they tell their child the story.

And if you feel stuck, that is what I am here for. Sometimes talking it through with someone who has walked alongside many families through this exact decision is all you need.
