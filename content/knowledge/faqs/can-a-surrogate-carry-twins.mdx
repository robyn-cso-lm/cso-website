She can, but most Canadian clinics now recommend transferring one embryo at a time because twin pregnancies carry higher risks for the surrogate and the babies. Twins can still happen when an embryo splits, so your surrogacy agreement should cover multiples either way.

I know many intended parents dream of twins, especially after a long road. Here is my honest view:

- **Twin pregnancies** are more likely to involve preterm birth, high blood pressure and a C-section.
- **The clinic decides** how many embryos it will transfer, based on medical guidelines and your situation.
- **The surrogate must agree.** Openness to multiples is part of matching, and some surrogates prefer a single baby.
- **The agreement** should spell out extra expenses, bed rest and support if twins do arrive.

Two healthy pregnancies, one at a time, are often the kinder route for everyone.
