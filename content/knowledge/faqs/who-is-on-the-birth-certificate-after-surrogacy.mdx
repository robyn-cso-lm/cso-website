In most situations the intended parents are named as the legal parents, but how that happens depends on the province. In Ontario, intended parents can be recognized without a court order when the surrogate consents in writing after the birth. Other provinces, such as Alberta, generally use a court order, and Quebec has its own notary-based process.

Not every province uses the same process. You can read the laws yourself: Ontario's [Children's Law Reform Act](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/90c12), British Columbia's [Family Law Act](https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/11025_01) and Alberta's [Family Law Act](https://kings-printer.alberta.ca/documents/Acts/F04P5.pdf). Your lawyer will tell you which steps apply where your surrogate lives and delivers.
