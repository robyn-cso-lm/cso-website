Embryo donation is when people who have finished building their family give their remaining frozen embryos to someone else trying to have a baby. In Canada it is legal but must be altruistic: embryos cannot be bought or sold, and both donors and recipients go through consent, counselling and clinic screening.

For recipients, it can be a meaningful path when both eggs and sperm are a challenge. For donors, it is one of several choices for leftover embryos, alongside keeping them stored, donating to research, or choosing to let them thaw.

Some things people often do not expect:

- The child may have full genetic siblings in the donor family.
- Many families choose some level of openness, and counselling helps everyone think that through.
- A lawyer should draft an agreement covering parentage, contact and what happens to any unused embryos.

Your clinic will confirm its own screening and consent steps.
