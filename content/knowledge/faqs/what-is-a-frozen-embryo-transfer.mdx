A frozen embryo transfer, or FET, is when an embryo that was frozen after IVF is thawed and placed into the uterus of the intended mother or a gestational surrogate. In surrogacy, almost every transfer is a frozen one, because it lets the clinic prepare the surrogate's body on its own schedule.

How it usually goes:

1. **Preparation:** the surrogate takes medications (often estrogen, then progesterone) to build up her uterine lining, or the clinic times things to her natural cycle.
2. **Monitoring:** bloodwork and ultrasounds check that the lining is ready.
3. **Transfer day:** the embryo is thawed and placed through a thin catheter, guided by ultrasound. It is quick and usually needs no anesthesia.
4. **The wait:** a blood pregnancy test (the beta) follows about one to two weeks later.

Every clinic has its own protocol, and your clinic will confirm the exact steps.
