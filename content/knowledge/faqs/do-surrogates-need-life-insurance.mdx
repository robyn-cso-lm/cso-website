In practice, yes. Most Canadian surrogacy agreements require a term life insurance policy on the surrogate for the length of the journey, paid for by the intended parents, so her own family is protected. The amount and details are set out in the surrogacy agreement.

Why it matters: pregnancy is safe for most healthy women, but it is never risk free. A surrogate is taking on that risk for someone else, and her own children and partner deserve protection.

Things to sort out early:

- **Timing:** the policy should be in place before the embryo transfer.
- **Disclosure:** the application must say honestly that she is a gestational surrogate.
- **Beneficiary:** usually someone the surrogate chooses.
- **Other coverage:** talk about disability and any gaps in her health or work benefits too.

Your lawyers will confirm what your agreement requires.
